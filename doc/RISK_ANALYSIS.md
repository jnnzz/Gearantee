# Risk Analysis

## Campus Equipment Borrowing & Reservation System

| Risk | Probability | Consequence | Prevention | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conflicting or double equipment reservations | Mid | Two users may reserve the same equipment at the same time. | Check item availability before reservation and approval. | Cancel or reschedule the conflicting request, or assign another available item. |
| Incorrect equipment availability or status | High | Users may reserve equipment that is already borrowed, reserved, damaged, or under maintenance. | Automatically update equipment status after every transaction. | Verify the physical item and manually correct its status when needed. |
| Equipment damage, loss, or non-return | High | Other reservations may be affected, and the school may experience equipment loss or repair costs. | Record borrower, due date, and equipment condition before release. | Record the incident, mark the item unavailable/for repair, and follow school policy. |
| Unauthorized access or incorrect user permissions | Mid | Users may access functions or data that should only be available to custodians or administrators. | Apply secure login and role-based access control. | Disable affected accounts, correct permissions, and review affected records. |
| Data loss or database corruption | Mid | Equipment, borrower, reservation, or transaction records may be lost. | Perform backups and use proper database validation and constraints. | Restore the latest valid backup and recover important records. |
| SQL Server backup cannot be restored | Mid | A backup may exist but still be unusable during an incident. | Automate SQL Server backups, monitor results, retain copies separately, and perform scheduled restore tests. | Restore the latest verified backup, apply documented recovery steps, and reconcile transactions entered after the recovery point. |
| Entity Framework migration failure or schema drift | Mid | Deployment may fail or application code may not match the SQL Server schema. | Keep all schema changes in reviewed EF Core migrations, test against a staging database, and take a verified backup before production migration. | Stop deployment, preserve logs, restore the verified backup when necessary, and correct the migration rather than making undocumented SSMS changes. |
| System or server downtime | Mid | Users may be unable to reserve, approve, release, or return equipment. | Test deployment and maintain a stable server/database setup. | Record transactions manually and encode them once the system is restored. |
| Tailwind CSS production build omits required styles | Mid | Pages may be visually broken after deployment even though they worked during development. | Scan all Razor Views and JavaScript sources, avoid dynamically assembled class names, safelist unavoidable dynamic classes, and test the minified production output. | Rebuild with corrected content paths/safelist entries and redeploy the generated stylesheet. |
| Natural disaster (for example, severe typhoons, earthquakes, widespread power outage, or flooding) | Mid | The system may be disrupted or delayed due to damage and the effects of a natural calamity. | Not applicable to the event itself; maintain disaster-preparedness procedures for project and operations staff. | Maintain cloud synchronization of source code and database migrations; avoid single points of failure on local devices; configure remote/cloud-hosted database environments where approved. |
| Requirement changes or scope creep | Mid | Additional features may cause delays and increase the project workload. | Finalize and document agreed system functions before development. | Prioritize core requirements and move optional features to a future version. |
| Limited development experience of team members | High | Development errors, poor implementation, or delays may occur. | Assign tasks according to skills, research unfamiliar topics, and review work regularly. | Ask for guidance, simplify complex features, and redistribute tasks when needed. |
| Team member absence or delayed assigned tasks | Mid | Other modules may be delayed and workload may become uneven. | Assign clear responsibilities and monitor progress regularly. | Reassign critical tasks and prioritize essential system functions. |
| Incorrect or incomplete equipment/borrower information | High | Users may reserve the wrong item, unavailable items may appear borrowable, and reports may become unreliable. | Require item code, category, status, condition, and location; validate forms; limit edits to administrators; conduct regular inventory checks. | Correct the record, cancel/adjust affected reservations, notify affected borrowers, and perform a physical inventory review. |
| Data privacy breach | Low | Student/faculty contact details and borrowing history may be exposed, causing trust and policy issues. | Collect only necessary data; restrict access by role; use HTTPS, secure database credentials, audit logs, and retention rules. | Contain access, preserve evidence, notify school data/privacy officers, follow the institution’s incident procedure, and remediate the vulnerability. |
| Secrets committed to source control | Mid | SQL Server, SMTP, or authentication credentials may be exposed and reused by unauthorized parties. | Store development values in .NET user secrets and production values in protected hosting configuration; add secret scanning to reviews/CI. | Revoke and rotate the exposed values immediately, remove them from active configuration, review access logs, and follow the incident process. |
| Custodian fails to record release or return promptly | Mid | System status differs from the real physical location of equipment, producing incorrect availability. | Make release and return recording part of the handover checklist; provide a simple custodian queue and daily outstanding-loan review. | Reconcile with the physical item and borrower, update the transaction with audit notes, and conduct an end-of-day inventory check. |
| Insufficient user training or resistance to the new process | Mid | Staff may continue using manual records, enter inconsistent data, or bypass the system. | Provide role-specific guides, short training sessions, sample accounts, and a pilot phase with feedback. | Offer refresher training and support; simplify unclear screens; reconcile manual records while adoption is completed. |
